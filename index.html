Research

Master’s thesis · in progress, expected June 2027

An Investigation of Low Glucose and Insulin With or Without Adjunctive Ketone Therapy During Ex-Situ Heart Perfusion And Respective Functional And Myocardial Health Outcomes

Supervisor: Darren Freed, University of Alberta Department of Surgery. Funded by a Canada Graduate Research Scholarship from CIHR and the Walter H Johns Graduate Fellowship.

Donor hearts can be kept beating on a perfusion circuit while they wait for a recipient. What the heart is fed during that time affects how well it holds up. My work asks which metabolic conditions protect the heart, and how we can tell.

  • Glucose restrictionLimiting glucose during normothermic perfusion, and its effects on edema, mitochondrial integrity, oxidative stress and protein O-GlcNAcylation.
  • Ketones as a substrateTesting ketones as a less oxidative alternative fuel to glucose, and as an addition to low-glucose perfusate.
  • Perfusate compositionHemoglobin concentration, hemolytic byproducts and plasma proteins, and how each shapes function over long preservation.
  • Preservation methodsHypothermic machine perfusion, target temperature, and a review of donor heart preservation approaches.

I completed a BSc with Honours in Physiology at the University of Alberta in 2025 and have worked in the lab as a summer research assistant since 2023. I also mentor newer students in the lab on bench work such as western blots and ELISAs, and on cardiac surgery technique.
